# Independent Future Adaptation

The V0.x ladder is closed. The next major research object is a genuinely independent test of whether corrective topology measured now predicts adaptation to future correction demands that were not available during matching or freezing.

## 1. Flagship hypothesis

The central empirical claim is

$$
I\!\left(
\Phi(\mathfrak C_t,\Lambda_t);
\mathrm{FutureAdaptation}
\mid
Q_{\rm state},Q_{\rm adapt}
\right)>0.
$$

Here:

- $Q_{\rm state}$ measures present task capability;
- $Q_{\rm adapt}$ measures standard/ordinary adaptability;
- $\Phi(\mathfrak C_t,\Lambda_t)$ measures corrective topology and preserved lineage;
- $\mathrm{FutureAdaptation}$ is evaluated only after a previously unannounced correction demand is introduced.

The distinctive claim is not that better systems adapt better. It is that **corrective topology contains predictive information about later adaptation beyond present competence and ordinary adaptability**.

## 2. Strong matched prediction

Construct systems $A$ and $B$ such that

$$
Q_{\rm state}^{A}\approx Q_{\rm state}^{B}
$$

and

$$
Q_{\rm adapt}^{A}\approx Q_{\rm adapt}^{B},
$$

while deliberately separating

$$
\Phi_A\neq\Phi_B.
$$

Then freeze both systems before the future challenge is disclosed.

The prediction is

$$
Q_{\rm future}^{A}\neq Q_{\rm future}^{B}
$$

in the direction implied by preserved corrective topology.

A useful experimental compression is:

$$
\text{match present capability}
\rightarrow
\text{measure/separate }\Phi
\rightarrow
\texttt{FREEZE}
\rightarrow
\text{unannounced correction}
\rightarrow
\text{future adaptation}.
$$

## 3. What must be matched

The strongest control is not a weak baseline. It is an apparently equivalent system.

Before the challenge, treatment and control should be matched as closely as possible on:

- present task performance;
- standard adaptation/fine-tuning speed;
- model size / compute budget where applicable;
- available memory or context capacity;
- number of retained candidate policies if that is not itself the manipulated object;
- prior exposure to task families;
- immediate calibration or confidence metrics;
- ordinary robustness measures.

The benchmark should make these present-state metrics deliberately uninformative about the contrast of interest.

## 4. What should vary

The manipulated or measured object is the quality of corrective continuation space:

$$
\Phi_t=\Phi(\mathfrak C_t,\Lambda_t;P,h).
$$

Candidate dimensions include:

### Reachability

Which future corrective states can still be reached without violating the governing contract?

### Reconfigurability

Can present commitments be released, replaced, or recombined when new evidence arrives?

### Challengeability

Do independent channels remain by which reality can reduce confidence in the current interface/model?

### Reopenability

Can previously discarded candidates or representations be recovered when later evidence makes them relevant?

### Lineage

Are failed attempts, collision witnesses, provenance, and prior discriminating evidence still accessible when a later contradiction must be diagnosed?

### Authority flexibility

Can the system withhold or revise commitment when current evidence does not uniquely warrant a choice?

No single scalar should be assumed to summarize these dimensions before measurement validates such a reduction.

## 5. Future challenge

The future challenge must be hidden from the matching and freezing stage.

The critical causal ordering is

$$
\text{match/measure}
\rightarrow
\texttt{FREEZE}
\rightarrow
E_{t+1}^{\rm surprise}
\rightarrow
\text{adaptation}.
$$

Examples of admissible challenge structures include:

- a previously unannounced requirement change;
- a distributional shift that invalidates one retained representation but not another;
- a new constraint that makes a previously irrelevant candidate necessary;
- a contradiction requiring reopening of prior evidence;
- an interface change that invalidates a shortcut while preserving task semantics;
- a newly authorized obligation competing for finite capacity.

The challenge should not be chosen after observing which system has better corrective topology.

## 6. Primary future-adaptation endpoints

Raw final score is insufficient. The benchmark should measure the dynamics of correction.

Candidate endpoints:

$$
H_{\rm recover}
=
\text{eventual correction success},
$$

$$
T_{\rm recover}
=
\text{latency to warranted recovery},
$$

$$
C_{\rm revise}
=
\text{cost of revision},
$$

$$
R_{\rm collateral}
=
\text{damage to previously valid structure},
$$

$$
R_{\rm reopen}
=
\text{ability to reopen discarded alternatives},
$$

and

$$
\Delta V_{\rm future}
=
\text{post-correction viability change}.
$$

These endpoints should remain separate until evidence licenses aggregation.

## 7. STOP / abstention as an empirical prediction

V0.8 gives a concrete cross-domain prediction:

> **STOP is not necessarily failure when uniqueness has not been earned.**

A system may face evidence such that

$$
|\operatorname{Top}(\mathcal E)|>1.
$$

A forced policy commits anyway. An authority-disciplined policy preserves unresolved alternatives.

The external question is not whether STOP improves immediate score. It is whether withholding unjustified contraction improves later quantities such as

$$
R_{\rm reopen},
\quad
R_{\rm collateral},
\quad
H_{\rm recover},
\quad
T_{\rm recover}.
$$

This yields the testable claim:

> **Calibrated refusal under unresolved evidence can be an adaptive capability rather than a competence deficit.**

A lucky commitment does not count as correct authority merely because the future happened to favor it.

## 8. External observability / flux

The theory should not depend on privileged introspection into a hand-designed internal state.

A stronger scientific requirement is that corrective topology emits observable consequences.

Candidate observable signatures include:

- blocked correction path $\rightarrow$ predictable later failure mode;
- preserved alternative $\rightarrow$ lower-cost later revision;
- excess authority $\rightarrow$ premature commitment and reduced reopening;
- preserved collision lineage $\rightarrow$ faster mechanism diagnosis after recurrence;
- independent challenge channel $\rightarrow$ improved calibration under interface failure;
- generative collision handling $\rightarrow$ better subsequent probe selection.

The long-run goal is to infer where corrective capacity is being created, spent, blocked, or preserved from externally measurable behavior.

## 9. Falsification conditions

The flagship hypothesis should weaken or fail if, after adequate matching and measurement:

1. $\Phi$ adds no predictive information about future adaptation beyond $Q_{\rm state}$ and $Q_{\rm adapt}$;
2. apparent $\Phi$ effects disappear under independently held-out challenge families;
3. effects are explained entirely by hidden present-capability differences;
4. purported reopenability or lineage measures do not predict actual reopening or repair;
5. STOP/authority discipline preserves no future correction capacity once its immediate costs are accounted for;
6. the proposed topology metrics fail invariance or measurement-validity tests across admissible representations.

Negative results should update the architecture rather than be absorbed as implementation noise.

## 10. Preregistration discipline

Before running a serious cross-domain test, freeze:

- matching variables and tolerances;
- the candidate definition of $\Phi$;
- admissible measurement transformations;
- future challenge generation and holdout procedure;
- primary and secondary endpoints;
- authority for interpreting `NOT_IDENTIFIED`;
- missingness rules;
- collateral-damage and reopenability measurements;
- the scope within which a positive result may update the theory.

The benchmark should preserve the same rule learned in V0.x:

> **No post-hoc goalpost moving. Failure signals failure, not cause.**

## 11. Success criterion

The strongest result would show that among systems matched on present competence and ordinary adaptability,

$$
\Phi_A>\Phi_B
$$

predicts a pattern such as

$$
H_{\rm recover}^{A}>H_{\rm recover}^{B},
$$

$$
T_{\rm recover}^{A}<T_{\rm recover}^{B},
$$

and

$$
R_{\rm collateral}^{A}<R_{\rm collateral}^{B},
$$

under genuinely independent future correction demands.

That would move the program from synthetic localization to independent predictive consequence.
