# Architecture

## 1. Scientific object

Safe self-improvement is not defined as monotonic improvement in present score. The object is the evolution of a system's **corrective dynamics**: which future revisions remain reachable, what evidence can still distinguish them, what authority those distinctions may acquire, and which commitments preserve or destroy later correction.

The state object is:

\[
\mathcal K_t=(R_t,\mathfrak C_t,\Lambda_t).
\]

- \(R_t\): authoritative present representation/state.
- \(\mathfrak C_t\): candidate corrective continuation space.
- \(\Lambda_t\): lineage of collisions, failed transitions, provenance, and reopenable evidence.

For contract \(P\) and horizon \(h\):

\[
\mathfrak C_t=\operatorname{CorrCont}(R_t,P,h).
\]

Two systems can therefore satisfy:

\[
R_t^A\approx R_t^B
\]

while differing materially in:

\[
\mathcal K_t^A\neq\mathcal K_t^B
\]

because their reachable correction topology or preserved lineage differs.

## 2. Corrective-topology functional

Define:

\[
\Phi_t=\Phi(\mathfrak C_t,\Lambda_t;P,h).
\]

\(\Phi_t\) is deliberately not candidate count. It may include:

- **coverage** — which future obligations remain satisfiable;
- **reachability** — which successor states can still be reached;
- **reconfigurability** — whether current commitments can be released or transformed;
- **challengeability** — whether independent reality-contact can still alter confidence;
- **reopenability** — whether discarded alternatives can be recovered;
- **lineage** — whether the evidence required to diagnose earlier failure survives.

The synthetic ladder repeatedly falsified the shortcut:

\[
|\mathfrak C_t|\approx\Phi_t.
\]

Future Sufficiency is about reachable corrective structure, not raw multiplicity.

\(\Phi_t\) is a **structural descriptor**, not a primitive scalar “amount of optionality” and not an objective the theory currently licenses maximizing. Any scalar ordering or optimization objective over corrective topology requires additional declared assumptions about admissible futures, costs, and value.

## 3. Promoted corrective pipeline

The current promoted causal architecture is:

\[
W
\rightarrow
O
\rightarrow
\widehat\Phi
\rightarrow
\mathcal E
\rightarrow
\mathrm{Auth}
\rightarrow
\mathrm{Select}
\rightarrow
\Gamma
\rightarrow
H_{\rm future}.
\]

Interpretation:

1. **\(W\) — contact / collision witness.** Reality produces discrepancy, consequence, or constraint.
2. **\(O\) — interface.** The system preserves or collapses distinctions exposed by contact.
3. **\(\widehat\Phi\) — learned semantic map.** Relational structure is assigned predictive meaning for future transformability.
4. **\(\mathcal E\) — evidential state.** Point estimates, uncertainty, grounding lineage, stability, and candidate margins remain available for warrant.
5. **\(\mathrm{Auth}\) — authority allocation.** Evidence is translated into permission to commit, abstain, retain, revise, or reopen.
6. **\(\mathrm{Select}\) — commitment operation.** A candidate is chosen, or selection is withheld.
7. **\(\Gamma\) — reachable transition topology.** The selected commitment constrains which future states remain accessible.
8. **\(H_{\rm future}\) — future adaptation / correction consequence.** The system later encounters demands not exhausted by its present state.

Upstream success never automatically licenses downstream success.

Phase V, described below, is a **bounded research-layer semantic authority interface**. Its demonstrated result does not silently replace or extend this promoted pipeline.

## 4. Phase-V semantic authority boundary

The semantic-constitution lineage has reached a demonstrated architecture for consuming externally constituted semantic equivalence without converting it into ontology.

The operational signature is:

\[
\boxed{
\textbf{receive}
\rightarrow
\textbf{scope}
\rightarrow
\textbf{audit}
\rightarrow
\textbf{use}
\rightarrow
\textbf{stop}.
}
\]

The strongest current Phase-V principle is:

\[
\boxed{
\textbf{An equivalence relation carries authority only together with the jurisdiction that constituted it.}
}
\]

### 4.1 Purpose-indexed semantic equivalence

For external regime \(r\) and declared semantic purpose \(\kappa\):

\[
x\equiv_{r,\kappa}y
\]

means only that \(x\) and \(y\) are interchangeable under \(r\) for purpose \(\kappa\).

The semantic object is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
(\equiv_{r,\kappa},\kappa),
}
\]

not an unindexed equivalence relation.

### 4.2 Reference, equivalence, and identity are distinct

Maintain:

\[
\boxed{
=_{\rm ref}
\not\Rightarrow
\equiv_{r,\kappa}
\not\Rightarrow
\equiv_{I,S}.
}
\]

Also:

\[
\boxed{
\equiv_{r,\kappa}
\not\Rightarrow
\equiv_{r,\kappa'}.
}
\]

This blocks a recurring authority leak: a relation useful for one operation cannot silently acquire authority for another.

### 4.3 Equality of denotation lives in the denotation domain

A semantic-equivalence regime is represented by:

\[
\mathcal R^{\rm eq}
=
(r,\kappa,S_{\rm pres},D_r,\nu_r,\Omega_r),
\]

with:

\[
\nu_r:S_{\rm pres}\to D_r.
\]

Define:

\[
x\equiv_{r,\kappa}y
\iff
\nu_r(x)=_{D_r}\nu_r(y).
\]

The equality on the right belongs first to \(D_r\).

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\textbf{Equality of denotations does not automatically become identity of presentations.}
}
\]

### 4.4 Separate regime registries

Phase V maintains:

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak R_{\rm constituted}^{\rm eq}
\neq
\mathfrak R_{\rm constituted}^{\rm id}.
}
\]

Hence:

\[
\boxed{
r\in\mathfrak R_{\rm constituted}^{\rm eq}
\not\Rightarrow
r\in\mathfrak R_{\rm constituted}^{\rm id}.
}
\]

The same distinction applies to audited regimes.

### 4.5 Congruence is a separate burden

A relation may be semantically constituted without being preserved by every consumer.

For licensed operation family \(\Omega_r\), the congruence audit asks whether:

\[
x\equiv_{r,\kappa}y
\Rightarrow
f(x)\equiv_{r,\kappa'}f(y)
\]

for each declared operation \(f\in\Omega_r\).

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{equivalence}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{congruence}.
}
\]

### 4.6 Congruence does not imply future sufficiency

Even a valid local quotient may erase distinctions needed by a later future obligation.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{congruence}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{future sufficiency}.
}
\]

The downstream sequence is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{semantic regime}
\rightarrow
\text{purpose-indexed equivalence}
\rightarrow
\text{congruence scope}
\rightarrow
\text{licensed quotient}
\rightarrow
\text{future sufficiency}
\rightarrow
\text{corrective reachability}.
}
\]

Identity may appear as a separately constituted semantic jurisdiction; it is not forced to precede every useful equivalence.

### 4.7 Cross-regime comparison requires constituted alignment

Two equivalence regimes are not automatically comparable.

Before comparison, establish:

1. purpose/proposition compatibility;
2. carrier alignment;
3. relation/operation typing compatibility;
4. target-independent transport.

For different carriers:

\[
\tau_r:S_r\to S_\ast
\]

is itself a semantic object requiring authority.

Only then:

\[
\widetilde{\equiv}_r
=
(\tau_r\times\tau_r)(\equiv_r)
\]

may enter a cross-regime invariant such as:

\[
\equiv_\Box^{\rm cross}
=
\bigcap_r\widetilde{\equiv}_r.
\]

If alignment is not constituted, the correct status is:

```text
JURISDICTIONAL_DIVERGENCE
```

not `REGIME_DISAGREEMENT`.

Permanent rule:

\[
\boxed{
\textbf{unlicensed alignment}
\not\Rightarrow
\textbf{cross-regime comparison}.
}
\]

## 5. Three corrective jurisdictions

### Future Sufficiency (FS)

Question:

> Does the current pipeline preserve the distinctions and reachable transformations still required for future correction?

FS is contract- and horizon-relative:

\[
FS_{P,h}(R_t).
\]

A currently adequate state can still be Future-Insufficient if it has consumed the transition topology needed for later authorized correction.

### Contradiction-Correction Architecture (CCA)

Question:

> What does the evidence actually warrant changing, preserving, distinguishing, or reopening?

CCA prevents failure signals from acquiring causal meaning automatically:

\[
\text{failure}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{cause identified}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{repair authorized}.
\]

### Controlled Adaptive Reasoning System (CARS)

Question:

> What authority may an evidence-supported distinction acquire and retain in commitment?

CARS prevents outcome success from laundering unjustified commitment into warrant:

\[
\text{correct outcome}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{correct authority}.
\]

Phase V is compatible with these jurisdictions but does not collapse into them. It concerns the authority contract under which an external semantic relation may enter downstream reasoning at all.

## 6. Dual failure laws

### Premature merging

Let \(O\) be an interface and \(T_{\rm future}\) the downstream property on which correction depends.

A sufficient interface must satisfy:

\[
\ker O\subseteq\ker T_{\rm future}.
\]

Violation:

\[
\ker O\not\subseteq\ker T_{\rm future}.
\]

Then there exist \(x,y\) such that:

\[
O(x)=O(y),
\qquad
T_{\rm future}(x)\neq T_{\rm future}(y).
\]

The interface has merged alternatives whose future corrective consequences remain distinct.

> **Do not merge what future correction still distinguishes.**

### Unauthorized splitting

Let \(\mathcal E\) be the evidential state and \(A\) the authority/commitment map.

A warranted authority map must not discriminate more finely than the evidence licenses:

\[
\ker\mathcal E\subseteq\ker A.
\]

Violation:

\[
\ker\mathcal E\not\subseteq\ker A.
\]

Then the commitment layer distinguishes alternatives that the evidence still treats as non-unique.

> **Do not distinguish in commitment what present evidence still merges.**

These are dual errors:

- **premature merge:** erase a distinction reality still requires;
- **unauthorized split:** manufacture a distinction the evidence has not earned.

Phase V adds a third recurrent hazard:

- **unauthorized transfer:** consume a valid distinction/equivalence outside the semantic jurisdiction that constituted it.

Compactly:

\[
\boxed{
\textbf{No downstream convenience may create upstream semantic authority.}
}
\]

\[

## 8. Transition semantics

Selection changes not only the present state but the topology of reachable successors.

Write:

\[
S_1\xRightarrow[P,E]{}S_2
\]

for a licensed transition under contract \(P\) and evidence \(E\), and:

\[
\Gamma_P(S_1,E)
=
\{S_2:S_1\xRightarrow[P,E]{}S_2\}
\]

for the reachable successor set.

The key empirical separation is:

\[
\text{present sufficiency}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{future transformability}.
\]

The selection operation and the interface supplied to it are therefore themselves Future-Sufficiency loci.

Phase V makes the upstream side equally explicit:

\[
\text{semantic equivalence}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{permission to consume the quotient for arbitrary future operations}.
\]

## 9. Governing invariant

> **No committed transformation may consume the evidential, representational, or authority pathway required to correct that transformation later.**

Operationally:

> **Preserve distinctions until their future consequences are exhausted; grant commitment authority only to the level the evidence identifies.**

This is not a preservation-maximalist rule. It does not authorize retaining arbitrary structure merely because it might matter someday.

The Phase-V semantic form is:

> **Receive external semantic authority under an explicit scope; preserve its type and jurisdiction; use only what that contract licenses; stop where it stops.**

Together:

\[
\boxed{
\textbf{correctability requires both preserved distinctions and disciplined authority transfer.}
}
\]
